A minimal real-time viewer for four standalone character models.

### How the models are made

The characters are **not** downloaded datasets and they are not sculpted by
hand in a DCC tool. They are generated by the pipeline in `tools/`, which
uses the same implicit-surface technique that procedural character tools
such as Blender's metaballs / geometry-nodes volumes and SDF modelling use:

1. **Signed distance field.** Every body volume — head, jaw, neck, torso,
   arms, legs, uniform, skirt, socks, shoes — is defined as an analytic
   distance primitive (`tools/chibi/sdf.py`).
2. **Smooth booleans.** Limbs are merged into the torso with a *smooth
   minimum* (Quilez polynomial `smin`), so an arm grows out of the shoulder
   instead of being a cylinder planted next to it. Garments are shells that
   cut through the skin they cover, which removes the double surface that
   otherwise makes thin cloth break apart during extraction.
3. **Isosurface extraction.** The field is sampled on a regular grid
   (5 mm voxels) and turned into a single watertight mesh with **Surface
   Nets**, a dual method that produces far more even triangles than
   marching cubes. Extracted vertices are then pulled back onto the zero
   level set with Newton iterations, which removes the concentric banding a
   raw dual mesh leaves on curved surfaces.
4. **Material assignment.** Each vertex is assigned to the group that owns
   the surface there, measured from the hard (non-blended) distance of each
   material group, with a small inward offset along the normal and a
   majority vote over the vertex neighbourhood.
5. **Cloth and props.** Hair, facial features, the sailor collar, the
   ribbon and the instruments stay in the mesh layer, where detail is
   finer than a voxel. The sailor collar is not a separate blob: its
   surface is *probed out of the body field* and offset a few millimetres
   outwards, so it follows the shoulder line exactly
   (`tools/chibi/garment.py`).
6. **Export.** A dependency-free glTF 2.0 / GLB writer
   (`tools/chibi/glb.py`) writes one primitive per material with sRGB
   colours converted to linear space.

The result is a single continuous body per character rather than a pile of
interpenetrating primitives, which is what separates an authored model
from assembled geometry.

### Rebuilding the models

```bash
python3 tools/build_characters.py                     # all four
python3 tools/build_characters.py --only hitori       # one character
python3 tools/build_characters.py --spacing 0.004     # finer voxels
```

Requirements: Python 3 with NumPy. No other third-party packages, and no
network access, are needed.

Render the six-angle QA sheet for a model with:

```bash
python3 tools/preview/capture.py --model hitori-gotoh.glb --out /tmp/preview
```

`tools/test_mesh.py` runs the geometry-kernel unit tests.

### Licensing and source material

These models are a fan-made, procedurally generated interpretation of the
characters from *Bocchi the Rock!* (ぼっち・ザ・ロック！). The anime and
its characters are the property of their respective rights holders. The
models are original work produced by the generator in `tools/`; no
third-party mesh, texture or scan is redistributed with them. They are
included as a demonstration of the asset pipeline.
